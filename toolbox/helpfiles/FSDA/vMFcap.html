vMFcap

vMFcap computes the boundary of a von Mises-Fisher confidence cap in any dimensio

expand all in page

Syntax

  • P=vMFcap(mu,kappa)example
  • P=vMFcap(mu,kappa,conflev)example
  • P=vMFcap(mu,kappa,conflev,npts)example
  • [P, talpha]=vMFcap(___)example

Description

vMFcap is the directional-data counterpart of a Gaussian confidence ellipse: because the vMF(mu,kappa) density depends on y only through the inner product mu'*y (equivalently, the angle between y and mu), its iso-density contours are exactly the spherical caps {y in S^(p-1) : angle(y,mu) <= theta}, for any embedding dimension p.

vMFcap returns npts points on the boundary of the cap whose probability under vMF(mu,kappa) equals conflev, i.e. it returns the boundary of {y : mu'*y >= t_alpha} where P(mu'*y >= t_alpha) = conflev, together with the threshold t_alpha.

For p=3 the boundary is a genuine circle (a 1-sphere embedded in R^3) and vMFcap returns it exactly, as npts evenly spaced points. For p=2 the boundary is made of the two end points of an arc. For p>3 the boundary is a (p-2)-sphere in the (p-1)-dimensional subspace orthogonal to mu; vMFcap returns npts points drawn uniformly on that submanifold (in the native R^p) as a Monte Carlo representation. No dimensionality reduction is performed here: to show a cap together with the data use sphereplot (p=2, 3) or sphereprojplot (p>3).

Examples

expand all

  • 95 confidence caps for two vMF distributions on S^2 (p=3).

    • . A tight distribution (kappa=40) around [1 0 0] and a diffuse one (kappa=8) around [0 1 0]. The caps are exact circles. 

    mu1=[1 0 0]; kappa1=40;
mu2=[0 1 0]; kappa2=8;
P1 = vMFcap(mu1,kappa1);
P2 = vMFcap(mu2,kappa2);
sphereplot([], 'cap', {P1,P2}, 'center', [mu1; mu2], ...
'labels', {'kappa=40','kappa=8'});

  • Simulated data with their confidence cap.

    • . The cap at level 0.9 is superimposed to 500 units generated from the same distribution; about 90 per cent of them are inside. 

    rng(1)
mu = [0 0 1]; kappa = 15;
Y = vMFrnd(500, mu, kappa);
[P, talpha] = vMFcap(mu, kappa, 0.9);
inside = Y*mu' >= talpha;
sphereplot(Y, 'group', 2-inside, 'cap', {P}, 'center', mu, ...
'labels', {'inside','outside'});
title(sprintf('%.1f%% of the units inside the 90%% cap', 100*mean(inside)))

  • Effect of confidence level on the cap angular radius (p=3).

    • . Higher conflev gives a larger cap (wider angular radius). 

    mu = [0 0 1];   kappa = 15;
for cl = [0.50, 0.90, 0.95, 0.99]
[~, talpha] = vMFcap(mu, kappa, cl, 0);
fprintf('conflev=%.2f: theta_alpha = %.2f deg\n', cl, acosd(talpha));
end
    conflev=0.50: theta_alpha = 17.49 deg
conflev=0.90: theta_alpha = 32.17 deg
conflev=0.95: theta_alpha = 36.84 deg
conflev=0.99: theta_alpha = 46.13 deg

  • High-dimensional case (p=300): coverage and projection.

    • . The threshold of the cap is computed in dimension 300 and it is checked on simulated data. The data and the cap are then shown with sphereprojplot. 

    rng(2)
p = 300;
mu = zeros(1,p); mu(1) = 1;
kappa = 500;
Y = vMFrnd(5000, mu, kappa);
[P, talpha] = vMFcap(mu, kappa, 0.95, 300);
fprintf('Proportion of units inside the 95%% cap: %.4f\n', mean(Y*mu' >= talpha));
sphereprojplot(Y, 'cap', {P}, 'center', mu);
    Proportion of units inside the 95% cap: 0.9456

    Related Examples

    expand all

  • Verify that boundary points have unit norm and lie at angle theta_alpha.

    • For p=3, all points should be exactly on the boundary circle. 

    mu = [1 0 0]; kappa = 10; conflev = 0.95;
    [P, talpha] = vMFcap(mu, kappa, conflev, 200);
    norms    = vecnorm(P, 2, 2);           % should all be 1
    cosTheta = P * mu';                    % should all equal talpha
    fprintf('norm range: [%.10f, %.10f]\n', min(norms), max(norms));
    fprintf('mu''*P range: [%.10f, %.10f], talpha=%.10f\n', min(cosTheta), max(cosTheta), talpha);

  • kappa=0: the uniform distribution in different dimensions.

    • The cap with probability 0.95 covers almost the whole sphere for p=3; in high dimension the uniform distribution concentrates around the "equator" orthogonal to mu and the threshold approaches 0. 

    for p = [3 10 100 1000]
        [~, talpha] = vMFcap([1 zeros(1,p-1)], 0, 0.95, 0);
        fprintf('p=%4d: talpha = %.4f\n', p, talpha);
    end

    Input Arguments

    expand all

    mu — Mean direction. Vector.

    1-by-p unit vector; the centre of the vMF distribution whose confidence cap is requested.

    Data Types: single| double

    kappa — Concentration parameter. Scalar.

    kappa>=0. kappa=0 gives the uniform distribution on S^(p-1).

    Data Types: single| double

    Optional Arguments

    conflev — Confidence level of the cap. Scalar in (0,1).

    Default 0.95.

    The cap boundary is the set of directions whose angular distance from mu equals acos(t_alpha), where t_alpha is the (1-conflev) quantile of the marginal distribution of mu'*y under vMF(mu,kappa). See "More About" for the formula.

    Example: 0.90

    Data Types: double

    npts — Number of points used to represent the boundary. Scalar.

    Default 100. For p==3 these are evenly spaced around the exact circle (the first and the last coincide). For p==2 the rows alternate between the two end points of the arc.

    For p>3 they are a random uniform sample of the boundary (p-2)-sphere in the subspace orthogonal to mu. With npts=0 no point is generated (and no random number is used): this is useful when only the threshold talpha is needed.

    Example: 200

    Data Types: double

    Output Arguments

    expand all

    P —Boundary points. Matrix

    npts-by-p matrix of unit vectors on the boundary of the confidence cap. For p==3 all rows are exactly on the circle in S^2; pass P to sphereplot (option cap). For p>3 P is a Monte Carlo sample of the boundary (p-2)-sphere; see sphereprojplot.

    talpha —Threshold of the cap. Scalar

    The cap is the set of unit vectors y with mu'*y>=talpha, and acos(talpha) is its angular radius.

    Additional Details

    ANGULAR RADIUS OF THE CAP The marginal distribution of t = mu'*y under vMF(mu,kappa) has density proportional to (Mardia and Jupp, 2000, Section 9.3):

    g(t) = exp(kappa*t) * (1 - t^2)^((p-3)/2), t in [-1,1].

    The cap threshold t_alpha satisfies P(t >= t_alpha) = conflev, i.e.

    t_alpha is the (1-conflev) quantile of g. The angular radius is then theta_alpha = acos(t_alpha).

    For p=3 the exponent (p-3)/2 = 0 and g reduces to exp(kappa*t), giving the closed form t_alpha = 1 + log( (1-conflev) + conflev*exp(-2*kappa) ) / kappa, which is evaluated with log1p and expm1 to be accurate also for small kappa. For kappa=0 and p=3, t is uniform on [-1,1] (Archimedes) and t_alpha = 1 - 2*conflev. Note that t is uniform ONLY for p=3: for kappa=0 and p~=3 the density of t is (1-t^2)^((p-3)/2) and the general procedure is used.

    For p=2 the angle theta between y and mu has the von Mises density, proportional to exp(kappa*cos(theta)) on (-pi,pi], which is integrated numerically; t_alpha is the cosine of the half width of the arc centred at mu with probability conflev.

    For p>3 there is no closed form and g is integrated numerically. The computation is done on the logarithm of g, log g(t) = kappa*t + ((p-3)/2)*log(1-t^2), on a grid which is progressively restricted to the interval where g is not negligible. Working with log g is essential in high dimension: for p in the hundreds g itself underflows to 0 over the whole interval (for example (1-t^2)^((p-3)/2) is of the order of 1e-400 for p=768 and t=0.96), and it is concentrated on an interval which is too narrow to be found by a general purpose quadrature rule on [-1,1].

    BOUNDARY PARAMETRISATION Once t_alpha = cos(theta_alpha) is known, every point on the boundary is P_i = cos(theta_alpha)*mu + sin(theta_alpha)*v_i where v_i is a unit vector in the (p-1)-dimensional subspace orthogonal to mu. This satisfies ||P_i|| = 1 (unit sphere) and mu'*P_i = cos(theta_alpha) = t_alpha for all i (exactly on the cap boundary). For p=3 the subspace is 2-dimensional and v_i = cos(phi_i)*e1 + sin(phi_i)*e2 traces the exact circle as phi_i sweeps [0, 2*pi]. For general p, v_i is drawn uniformly from the (p-2)-sphere by projecting Gaussian noise off mu.
