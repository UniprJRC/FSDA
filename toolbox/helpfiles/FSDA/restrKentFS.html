restrKentFS

restrKentFS applies the concentration restriction to a set of Kent mixture component

expand all in page

Syntax

  • kappaout=restrKentFS(kappa, beta, r, dq, ni, restrfactor)example
  • kappaout=restrKentFS(kappa, beta, r, dq, ni, restrfactor, tol)example
  • [kappaout, betaout]=restrKentFS(___)example

Description

restrKentFS is the Kent-distribution analogue of restrKappaFS: it enforces the constraint max_j(kappa_j) / min_j(kappa_j) <= restrfactor across the groups, in the way which is optimal for the Kent likelihood. When the concentration kappa_j of a group has to be changed, its ovalness beta_j is rescaled proportionally, so that the ratio beta_j/kappa_j (which determines the shape, i.e. the eccentricity, of the contours of the group) is preserved and the condition beta_j < kappa_j/2 keeps holding. The restriction therefore acts on the "size" of the groups and leaves their shape and orientation untouched.

If the unrestricted estimates already satisfy the constraint they are returned unchanged. Otherwise, exactly as in restrKappaFS, the restricted estimates are the unrestricted ones truncated to an interval [m, restrfactor*m], and the optimal m is found by a 1-D minimisation of the (minus) log-likelihood (see "More About").

When restrfactor=1 all kappa_j are set to the same value. Groups with NaN kappa (empty/degenerate groups, as flagged by KentMstepFS) are passed through unchanged and do not take part in the restriction.

example

kappaout =restrKentFS(kappa, beta, r, dq, ni, restrfactor) Two Kent groups with very different concentration.

example

kappaout =restrKentFS(kappa, beta, r, dq, ni, restrfactor, tol) restrfactor=1: common kappa.

    Input Arguments

    expand all

    kappa — Concentration parameters. k-by-1 vector.

    Unrestricted estimates from KentMstepFS. Entries may be NaN (empty group).

    Data Types: single| double

    beta — Ovalness parameters. k-by-1 vector.

    Unrestricted estimates from KentMstepFS.

    Data Types: single| double

    r — Mean resultant lengths. k-by-1 vector.

    r(j) is the (weighted) mean of gamma3_j'*y over the units of group j, as returned by KentMstepFS (6th output).

    Data Types: single| double

    dq — Differences of second moments. k-by-1 vector.

    dq(j) is the (weighted) mean of (gamma1_j'*y)^2-(gamma2_j'*y)^2 over the units of group j, as returned by KentMstepFS (7th output). r and dq are the sufficient statistics of (kappa_j,beta_j) for given orientation.

    Data Types: single| double

    ni — Group sizes. k-by-1 vector.

    Number of units (or sum of the posterior probabilities) of each group. They are the weights of the groups in the likelihood.

    Data Types: single| double

    restrfactor — Restriction factor. Scalar >= 1.

    Maximum allowed ratio max(kappa_j)/min(kappa_j). restrfactor=1 forces a common kappa.

    Data Types: single| double

    Optional Arguments

    tol — Relative tolerance for the 1-D minimisation. Scalar.

    Default 1e-8.

    Example: 1e-10

    Data Types: double

    Output Arguments

    expand all

    kappaout —Restricted concentration parameters. k-by-1 vector, with max(kappaout)/min(kappaout) <= restrfactor

    NaN for empty/degenerate groups.

    betaout —Restricted ovalness parameters. k-by-1 vector

    betaout(j) = kappaout(j)*beta(j)/kappa(j).

    Additional Details

    LOSS FUNCTION For given orientation, minus the log-likelihood of group j divided by n_j is log c(kappa_j,beta_j) - kappa_j*r_j - beta_j*dq_j.

    Along the ray beta_j = e_j*kappa_j, with e_j = beta_j/kappa_j fixed at its unrestricted estimate, this is the function of one variable f_j(kappa) = log c(kappa, e_j*kappa) - kappa*(r_j + e_j*dq_j), which is convex (log c is the log-partition function of an exponential family, hence jointly convex in (kappa,beta)) and is minimised by the unrestricted estimate kappa_j. Therefore, for a given m, the minimiser of f_j over the interval [m, restrfactor*m] is kappa_j truncated to that interval, and the restricted estimates are found by minimising L(m) = sum_j n_j * f_j( min( max(kappa_j,m), restrfactor*m ) ) with respect to m. L is convex in m, and its minimiser lies in the interval [min_j kappa_j, max_j kappa_j / restrfactor], which is the search interval used by fminbnd.
